### Running the pilot and fixing the AI answers

The client was evaluating an AI-powered scientific-literature platform under licensed-content constraints. I ran the pilot in increments with 60 power users before the wider launch. Each increment reached them through demonstrations, workshops, Q&A and train-the-trainer sessions, and we collected their feedback after every one. Part of the pilot was moving about 700,000 documents from the previous vendor in 40 days, in phases, so the platform could go live with the first batches while the remaining deltas were processed.

When the AI answers fell short of what the client expected mid-pilot, I kept the client’s bar where it was and worked on the answers. I ran evaluation sessions with the client on answer quality, accuracy, hallucination limits and speed, and worked with the engineers on guardrails, retrieval settings and the system prompt so that answers stayed grounded in licensed sources, with citations. We shortened the QA and demonstration cycle so the client could assess working software, and ran load and performance tests with the product team before scale-up.

We launched in July 2026, on the planned date. The engineers did the model development, and I ran the implementation across the client’s workstreams and our six internal teams. Colleagues later reused my implementation approach, along with the tracking framework and the presentation materials.

### A second rollout

In 2026 I also led discovery and the first stage for a second pharmaceutical client moving off a legacy literature system: about 150,000 documents cleaned, normalized and imported into three libraries, with AI search and summarization live for 30 power users in June 2026. I was assigned a few days before the first on-site discovery sessions, so much of that first week was about giving the client confidence in the approach and agreeing what we’d need from them.
