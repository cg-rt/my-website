### The situation

A global chemical company needed a content-management solution that fitted its internal workflows. The work extended from understanding those requirements to launching the platform and preparing an operations team to run it.

The project spanned 36 months. A working implementation had to include the people, knowledge and handover needed to sustain it after launch.

### My responsibility

I carried the implementation through requirements scoping, system and workflow design, configuration, testing, training, launch, quality assurance and handover.

I worked with the client to translate its requirements into a practical configuration and delivery sequence. Testing and client feedback were part of the implementation, so the team could check that the solution matched the workflows it needed to support.

Training covered both users and administrators. Those audiences needed different levels of understanding: one to work with the platform, the other to support and maintain its operation. The handover connected that training with the documentation and knowledge the operational team would need.

### What changed

The system was delivered, launched and handed over. The client's operations team ran the platform independently after handover.

For this project, that was the meaningful endpoint: the software was in use and the team responsible for it could take the work forward. The implementation covered the transition into operational ownership as well as the technical launch.
