### Starting with a technical account review

The client had been through several contacts on our side, had a list of unresolved technical and configuration issues, and had stopped giving honest feedback because earlier efforts hadn’t changed anything they could see. Promising that it would be different this time would have made it worse.

So I started with a technical account review: a methodical look at the configuration, the open issues and the history, written down and brought back to the client with a prioritized plan and a plain account of what had gone wrong. Every commitment became a tracked item with a visible status. When I needed engineering or product, I escalated internally and kept that complexity away from the client, because what they needed to see was things getting resolved. Over a few months our calls went from formal status updates to a normal working relationship, and the client agreed to new implementation work.

I’ve used the same approach on five strategic accounts that were strained when I took them on. The account-review template that came out of this work was later used by colleagues for their own reviews.
